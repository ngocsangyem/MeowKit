Prompt + Cursor

The mark is the two things already on the screen when you talk to an agent: the chevron you type after, and the block cursor that waits. The cursor gets two ears. Nothing is added after that.

01 · The two parts

Prompt
Chevron. A greater-than sign drawn as two bevelled arms. It is the shell prompt, the diff marker and the "next" arrow at once. 72 × 112 units.
Cursor
Cursor. A caret block, 120 × 146 units, with two 38-unit triangles cut from its top edge. The ears are negative space — nothing is drawn on top.
Together
Lock. 24 units of gap, the width of one chevron bevel. The chevron stays ink, the cursor takes the one accent, so the eye reads "prompt, then answer".

02 · On the grid

20-unit grid
Every vertex lands on the grid. All angles are 90° or 45°. No curve, no radius, no taper anywhere in the mark.

Small cut
Under 24px the chevron closes up, so it is dropped and the cursor stands alone — meow-symbol-small.svg. The ears still read at 16px.

03 · What the mark does not do

No face, no whiskers, no paw, no braces, no ASCII cat. No gradient, no glow, no outline, no rounded corners. The cursor never tilts and the chevron never becomes a character in a live font. Two shapes, two colours, one idea — the rest is the wordmark's job.
